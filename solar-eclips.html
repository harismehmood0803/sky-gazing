Solar Eclipse

☀️ Definition

A solar eclipse happens when the Moon moves between the Earth and the Sun, blocking sunlight from reaching Earth.

🌍 Alignment

It occurs only when the Sun, Moon, and Earth are perfectly aligned in a straight line.

🌑 Types

There are three main types of solar eclipses — total, partial, and annular.

🌕 Total Solar Eclipse

In a total eclipse, the Moon completely covers the Sun, and the day briefly becomes dark.

🌗 Partial Solar Eclipse

In a partial eclipse, only part of the Sun is covered by the Moon.

🌘 Annular Solar Eclipse

In this type, the Moon is farther from Earth, so it looks smaller and leaves a “ring of fire” around the Sun.

🕶️ Safety

It is dangerous to look directly at a solar eclipse without special eclipse glasses, as it can harm your eyes.

📅 Frequency

Solar eclipses happen at least twice a year, but total eclipses are rare at any single location.

🔭 Scientific Importance

Eclipses help scientists study the Sun’s corona and understand more about the Earth–Moon–Sun system.
